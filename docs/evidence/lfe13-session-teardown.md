## Site audit (round 6): diagnostics off the lifecycle path (D-87), and every text of the failure record in the bound

Round 5 (head `7f2ba50f`) requested changes on two findings. The owner's decision of 2026-10-24, relayed by the manager (request `user-het-lfsolve-20261022-1413`): *(P1) every diagnostic or logger call reachable from the driver loop and teardown runs off the driver, enqueued or non-blocking per D-82 ("diagnostics never gate lifecycle"), so a held logger never delays a stop, a step or retirement; one row per call site and a gated test holding the logger at each class of site. (P2) every field of the failure record (`rfContent` and any other Text or list field) is counted in the whole-record bound and shrunk by `fitRecord`, with a boundary test per field.* The owner then decided the sink contract (**D-87**, design `8ef451ce`, #527 amendment 6391430747, re-approved by marker 6391508380): *the writer is a session-owned thread, the driver only enqueues; at teardown, after retirement is published, a writer stuck in the sink is cancelled and not waited for; undelivered diagnostics are recorded in the bounded failure/evidence record (a count plus the first entries); a sink failure is evidence, never primary; rewrite audit row D9.* The row D9 of the round-3 sweep recorded the sink writes as "OK by T4: nothing the stop protocol needs waits on a diagnostic"; it was wrong (the driver waited on the sink inside its own loop), and it is rewritten here.

**Round 6 (head `fd71a0ff`) and this round's closure.** The review found three issues in the new writer, all closed by construction below: (a) a write the sink raised for was acknowledged like a success, so the diagnostic was missing from the exact undelivered record (`Run.hs`, `forwardOne`) → `DiagFailedWrite`, one commit that keeps the diagnostic *failed* and records the failure, and the cut-off counts failed and interrupted entries with the pending and in-flight ones, once; (b) the abandonment was caught only around the write, so it could arrive while the writer waited or between two writes and reach the process's uncaught-exception handler → **one handler at the thread's boundary wraps the whole loop** (`writerThread`); (c) the closure example did not wait for the writer to enter the sink → the driver is held at the cut-off (`CuttingOff`) until the writer is where the example means it to be, and **every writer example now synchronizes on an observed sink entry or exit** (the entry of the held message, the acknowledgement leaving the queue, the failure in the evidence, the writer's end).

### The writer as a state machine

States: *not started*, *idle* (waiting for a pending diagnostic or the cut-off), *writing* (claimed, in the sink), *delivered* (acknowledged), *failed-undelivered*, *cancelled-undelivered*, *abandoned*, *ended*. Every non-success outcome is counted once in the undelivered record (`deUndelivered`, the first entries, the cause).

**The construction (D-87's writer lifecycle, point by point).**

* *Start.* `startWriter` runs in the masked step after the outer continuation is installed (`registerSessionRelease` of the composition) and before the first diagnostic (the "session starting" note, committed after `onReady`). It is told at `WriterStarting` / `WriterStarted`; if it throws, that is a failure of the composition (the result `Left` goes through `finish`, T2's "driver throws" row), not a sink failure, `onReady` is not called and a writer that was never started has no thread to cancel. It is not in the stop set; the stop pass never touches it.
* *While the session runs.* The driver, callbacks, the stop pass, `requestStop`, `latchTerminalFailure` and the recovery commits only commit entries; only the writer calls the sink. The writer drains the queue **continuously, including during teardown** (this supersedes T4's suspension and the driver's final attempt). The queue keeps T4's bound however long the writer is stuck: its entries are the fixed set of transitions' diagnostics and two notes, no per-tick or per-update entry exists.
* *A sink that raises or is cancelled.* `DiagFailedWrite` (a synchronous failure: the diagnostic is kept, failed, in the same commit that records the failure) or `DiagSinkFailed` (the sink's own cancellation, a defect of the writer): evidence in `DiagEvidence` (the exact count and the first texts, with the cleanup failures its exception carried, paid for out of the single `EngineReportBytes` budget below); no fault, no stop, no verdict change, no `erPrimary`, no secondary-summary slot, no `erOmitted`. **After it the writer makes no further write to that sink** (T4: "without another diagnostic-to-that-sink obligation"): the diagnostics meant later stay in the queue and are counted as undelivered at the cut-off. The authored `print`/`warn` sink (`sxOutput`) is unchanged: its fault still ends the owner and the whole session.
* *The cut-off.* After every role's retirement is published (or committed as `stopping`) and every release has run, and before the outcome is read or an exception raised, `finish` commits the "session ended" note (unless cancelled), gives the writer `graceYields` (1000) scheduler yields (a poll with a plain STM read that always ends: it never waits on the sink, and nothing depends on how it ends), then makes **one recovery commit**, `DiagCutOff cause`: it closes the queue (`stDiagClosed`), records every entry not acknowledged (pending, claimed or in the sink) in the undelivered entry (the exact count, the first messages cut visibly to their share of the budget below, and the cause), and empties the queue. After it a diagnostic the session produces is counted there directly (`enqueue`), and a late `DiagClaim`/`DiagDone`/`DiagInterrupt`/`DiagSinkFailed` changes no outcome, status or history field (Model *counts a diagnostic meant after the cut-off directly…*). Each entry therefore ends as exactly one of delivered (its `DiagDone` committed before the cut-off) or undelivered (it may still have written bytes; no exactly-once promise). The entry is on every outcome kind and readable through `sessionOutcomeNow` and after the return.
* *Cancelling the writer.* `abandonWriter` closes the phase (once) and delivers `WriterAbandoned` (an asynchronous exception) from a **helper thread**, as the foundation's `requestCancel` does, because delivery can block as long as the writer is in an uninterruptible region or a foreign call; the requester never blocks. No `throwTo`/`killThread` on the driver, no cancel-and-wait, no `withAsync` scope, no worker-group drain. On a cancellation of the caller the same steps run at once and the original cancellation escapes (T5): a cancellation that lands during the (masked) cut-off is deferred to the end of `finish`.
* *After the return (V-3).* The writer and the helper can outlive the call, so the logger stays borrowed until they end: `sessionDiagnosticsEnded :: Session -> STM Bool` (new, additive, on the existing handle; no host seam) says when, so the assembly that owns the logger can join it before releasing it; a sink stuck for good is then the application quit watchdog's case. Neither thread is a supervised worker, so their end, failure or cancellation never reaches the driver as a supervision delivery, never becomes the exception that propagates and never makes a report on the driver's thread. The README's state table documents that the writer can outlive the call and who observes its end. (The bare `forkIOWithUnmask` is deliberate: the worker group drains its workers with no deadline and no detach, the opposite of a sink the engine does not own.)

**What this supersedes.** D-82/T4's "only the driver outside owner callbacks runs diagnostic effects", the suspension of forwarding once teardown begins and "on a non-cancelled final path the driver makes one protected attempt at each pending diagnostic", and "a synchronous sink failure becomes a bounded engine failure" (for the diagnostic sink only). What T4 still requires holds and is tested: stop obligations are serviced ahead of diagnostics; a diagnostic has a stable identity, one claimant and the states *pending*, *in flight*, *interrupted*; the queue is bounded; a sink failure makes no diagnostic of its own; an interrupted or abandoned write is not retried and no exactly-once output is promised; the bounded failure record is readable without a sink.

### The diagnostics' evidence, and what it is not

`SessionOutcome.outDiagnostics :: DiagEvidence`: `deSinkFailures`/`deSinkFirst` (a sink that raised or was cancelled: the exact count and the first texts), `deUndelivered`/`deUndeliveredFirst` (the exact count and the first messages), `deCause`. It is evidence: it never changes `outVerdict`, never becomes `erPrimary`, never closes the session, takes no secondary-summary slot and no part of `erOmitted`, and a failure the session already has stays the primary. The count is exact and multibyte text is cut visibly at a code point (Model *cuts an undelivered message that is multibyte visibly…*). Because the last notes ("session stopping", "session ended") are written by a thread the call does not wait for, an ordinary session whose sink is slow can return with one of them recorded as undelivered; that is what D-87 asks for ("never dropped silently"), and nothing is wrong when it happens.

**One bound for every text: `EngineReportBytes`, for every validated profile (round 7's first finding).** Every path that keeps a text of the engine report or of the evidence goes through `Failure.hs`, and they share one budget:

| Path | Where | What it keeps | Bound |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| the report's first entry (a failure of any kind, a role failure's entry, the terminal failure's entry) | `noteEngine` | one message | `primaryBytes = min RoleFailureRecordBytes (EngineReportBytes − SecondarySummaries × SecondarySummaryBytes)` (zero when the summaries fill the report) |
| the report's secondary summaries (cleanup failures, later failures, a role failure's secondary entries, evidence an owner's end carried) | `noteEngine` | at most `SecondarySummaries` messages | `SecondarySummaryBytes` each, so `SecondarySummaries × SecondarySummaryBytes` in all; the rest are counted in `erOmitted` |

where `evidenceBytes = EngineReportBytes − (primaryBytes + SecondarySummaries × SecondarySummaryBytes)` is what the report can never use (zero when the summaries fill it), and `evidenceShare = evidenceBytes / (undelivered entries + failed-write entries + 1)`. A text is cut visibly to its share, or not kept at all when the share is nothing; the counts are exact in every case. Role failures (`outFailures`, `outTerminal`) are records, bounded by `RoleFailureRecordBytes` each (the table below), not part of the report. ### B. Every example of `Test.LuaEngine.Session.*`, and the event its assertions wait for

**Legend.** **P(x)**: `awaitPoint x`, a probe told after the commit it reports (its log is read only after P, after the call returned, or while the telling thread is held). **Ph(role,phase)**: `awaitPhase`. **St(c)**: `awaitStatus` on the condition. **Ret**: `awaitRetired`. **Reg(µs)**: `awaitRegistration`, the timer registration of that length is live, so a one-shot clock wake cannot be lost. **Line(l)**: the clock is moved and the timers woken *until* the output line l appears (`passTime`/`pumpUntil`). **Sink(m)**: the logger or output holds the entry m. **Gate**: a pool read is held at its test gate (`awaitReadOf`). **Join**: the outcome is read only after `runSession` returned and the scenario thread joined (`runScenario` returns after the call). **Held**: a negative check ("not yet") made only while the telling thread, owner or sink is parked. **Pure**: no thread, clock, probe or I/O.

Round 10's sweep found and fixed these shapes (every one in the table below is marked): **un-gated injected defect** (Failures *ends the whole session when a provider fails*: the faulty read now waits at the pool's gate until both roles run); **outcome read before the second request committed** (Evidence *ranks an interruption over a normal quit*: the call is held at the cut-off until both `requestStop`s returned); **output counts** (Clocks ×5, Failures ×2, Diagnostics ×2, Startup ×1, Commit ×2: each waits for the named line instead); **an attempt count** (Diagnostics: the two start notes by name); **one-shot clock wakes with no registration awaited** (nine, now `nudgeArmed`, which awaits the registration of the stated length first; the end-of-limits example pumps until both roles retired); **a negative check with nothing proving the waiter was parked** (host Windows: now `awaitBlockedOnSTM`); **1,000 yields** (host Creation: now the hook's own signal). `runScenario` answers the outcome the call returned, and joins the scenario thread after it; an assertion on a flag only a scenario request can set (`outQuit`, `outInterrupted`, the verdict of an interruption) is made only where that request is what ends the session (Failures *how a session ends*, Stop *takes no slot to stop* after its four stoppers joined, Evidence *ranks an interruption over a normal quit*, now held at the cut-off). Yields remain only inside condition loops that re-read their condition (`awaitRegistration`, `awaitSeen`, the `passTime` pump); no assertion depends on a yield count.

## Round 11 (final): the diagnostics holder publishes `entered` from inside the region it announces

Round 10's review (head `f98097da`) found one race: `Diagnostics.hs` `holdingWith` published `entered` **before** entering `uninterruptibleMask_`, so an example that saw the entry could still stop or fail the session while the writer was unmasked, and the abandonment helper could cancel it before its gate wait began; the `sessionDiagnosticsEnded is False` assertions of the uninterruptible variant could then fail though production was correct. **Fix**: the region is entered first and the signal and the gate wait are both inside it (`(if uninterruptibly then uninterruptibleMask_ else id) $ do publish; wait`), so the observed event guarantees the masking state the example needs.
