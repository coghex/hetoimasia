## Round 7 follow-up (owner directive, round 8): R1 and R2, enforced once

Round 7 (head `fa455d1c`, Codex, CHANGES_REQUESTED) found a preparation whose credits were refunded while it still ran, and a `Busy` handoff refusal that suppressed the region for good. Both are instances of the two rules below, so the rules are enforced in one place each.

**R1. Credits are released only at the owner's safe point.** `stateRunning` records the request whose preparation the owner is running (its captured values are used until it ends). `withdrawRecord` is the one function that issues `R.Withdraw`, and `retireArenaRecord` the one that issues `R.RetireArena`; while the matching preparation runs they defer: the request leaves the table (its outcome is decided in the transaction that found it) but becomes a ghost in `stateGhosts` that keeps its model record, its reserved service record and its command slot charged (the counting functions include ghosts), and the arena's retirement waits in `stateDeferredRetire`. `safePointSTM` (at the end of every preparation, on every exit path, and in the owner's terminal step) is the only other code that issues those events: it withdraws the ghosts, then retires the waiting arenas. Invariants added: the request being prepared always has its model record; a ghost or a deferred retirement exists only while a preparation runs.

Former release sites, now routed through the one function: `releaseReq` (called by `terminate`: explicit cancel, lease release, client end, closure of each request of an arena or of the service; and by `retireRequest`: taking a result), the removal's `retainResult` at commit, `resubmitSTM`'s withdraw before readmission, and `closeArena`'s `R.RetireArena`. Releases left as they were, with the reason: victim release, job acknowledgement and transfer outcomes run on the owner thread, so they cannot overlap its preparation; hold release and camera-demand events touch no request's credits.

**R2. A capacity refusal is never terminal.** `stateCapacityEpoch` advances on every event that can return a credit. Refusal sites changed or confirmed: (1) `BeginHandoff` refused `Busy`: previously marked the region as tried (never retried); now it stays a candidate and is suppressed only until the epoch moves (`stateHandoffBusy`); only a transfer that ran and failed is suppressed until the region changes, and a refusal that can never clear (the arena is gone) ends the attempt; (2) readmission of a superseded request (`resubmitSTM`): `Busy` waits, charged, and the retry pass reconsiders it (unchanged since round 6); (3) camera demand the model cannot admit stays pending in the model and `Service` reconsiders it; (4) admission returns `Busy` to the caller and creates nothing. Every other model refusal in the owner is a contract failure (a defect), not capacity.

Tests: `Credits` (new module): the preparation is held at `PointStaging` and the request is cancelled, its lease released, or the service closed; the model's banks and command slot are asserted unchanged until the gate opens and zero afterwards, with the invariants checked in between; and a transfer refused for want of staging capacity (a scan over staging sizes finds sizes where the refusal really happens, observed by the new `PointHandoffBusy` probe point) completes once the pending reads are cancelled. The randomized interleaving now has three groups: the plain 24 seeds; 24 seeds in which the owner itself injects a seeded cancellation, lease release or client end inside each preparation and checks the invariants there, with the credits held; and 16 seeds under capacity pressure (a bank holding about two regions and three commands) that end by granting every host and requiring every request to settle (bounded wait, so a request stuck on a refusal that never clears fails). After every step: `modelInvariants` and `chargeCheck` (no credit spent twice), the service invariants (no bank overfull: the retained bank and the command slots are checked against their limits), and no defect. Coverage is asserted per group (some request succeeds across the group), not per seed.

Negative controls: NC30 (a cancelled request gives its credits back during its preparation) fails all 3 `Credits` examples; NC31 (an arena is retired during its preparation) fails the lease-release and closure examples; NC32 (a capacity-refused transfer suppressed like a failed one) leaves the region dirty (bounded wait never returned); NC33 (the same as NC30, caught by the randomized property) fails 18 of 24 injected seeds ("the request being prepared has no model record"). The camera-demand example in `Budgets` now waits out a transient eviction refusal (R2: a read refused because the owner has not yet released an evicted region is temporary), which a 1-in-60 `-N4` run had shown.

## Round 8 follow-up (round 9, the last under the owner directive): the family, swept

Round 8 (head `6c45cd70`, Codex, CHANGES_REQUESTED) found that the capacity-refusal memory `stateHandoffBusy` was never retired when a transfer, region or arena ended, so it grew with every incarnation. The family is the owner's uncharged per-region side memory.

- **One write path, one forget path.** `rememberTried` and `rememberBusy` are the only writers, and write only for a region the model still holds in a live arena; `forgetRegionRetry` (completed transfer, released victim) and `forgetArenaRetry` (arena closure) are the only erasers, plus a wholesale clear in the owner's terminal step. The five former ad hoc sites (the `stateTransfers` deletes in `closeArena`, the victim release in `Owner.releaseVictim`, the owner terminal's victim retirement, the transfer's success, and the three inserts) now go through them. Writing only for live regions also closes a leak the randomized property found (two flaky seeds in 40 `-N4` runs): a transfer in flight when its arena ended re-added a memory after the closure had cleared it.
- **Swept, for the same shape of leak:** every other side table in the state has an owner and a bound: `stateNames` (arenas plus pending opens), `stateSnapshots` (outstanding borrows, charged), `stateRetiring` (until the model reports release), `stateGhosts` and `stateDeferredRetire` (cleared at the safe point; an invariant says they exist only while a preparation runs), `stateVictims` (consumed by the owner), `stateDefects` (ends the owner). An invariant now says no retry memory outlives its region, and `inspectRetryMemory` is zero after every randomized run.

Tests: `reconsiders a transfer refused for want of capacity ... and forgets it when the arena ends` now runs three incarnations on one service and asserts the retry memory is zero after each arena ends (the reviewer's scenario); `remembers nothing about a transfer whose arena ended while it was in flight`; the randomized interleaving asserts `inspectRetryMemory` is zero at the end of every seed. Negative controls: NC34 (the memory is never forgotten: the round-8 defect) leaves 1 entry; NC35 (writes not guarded by liveness) leaves 1 entry in the in-flight example.

## Round 9 follow-up (owner directive, round 10): every limit counts every resource-holding state

Round 9 (head `abece124`, Codex, CHANGES_REQUESTED) found that the service-wide arena cap counted live arenas and pending opens but not `stateRetiring`, so open / borrow / release-owner-lease / reopen kept five incarnations in the model against a limit of four while `inspectInvariants` returned `[]`. The family is *a limit's admission count that is not the set of entities holding the resource*.

- **One counting function per limit, and an independent enumeration that must agree.** `arenaUse`, `leasesInUse`, `slotsHeldHere`, `retainedBankUse` and the view and client sizes are the only counts the admission tests use. `limitRows` (exported as `inspectLimits`) pairs each with an enumeration that shares no code with it (the model's own arena list, each client's lease and view sets, the request and ghost tables); `invariants` requires them to agree and the count never to exceed the limit.
- **Every limit and the states it counts:**

| Limit | States counted (until the release has completed) |
| --- | --- |
| Arenas (4) | live arenas; pending opens (admitted, not settled); retiring arenas (closed, still held by a borrowed snapshot or an unfinished release), until the model reports the retirement complete. This was the defect. |
| Arena leases (64) | every lease record, live or revoked, until released; pending opens and attaches |
| Views (8) / Clients (256) | open views / registered clients, until closed / ended |
| Command slots | the model's accepted commands; untaken results; pending acquisitions; reads and removals waiting to be admitted again; ghosts (withdrawn while their preparation runs) |
| Retained bank | outstanding snapshots' backing; one result record per request from admission to take, ghosts included |
| Per-arena retained edits, resident / edit / staging banks, holds, jobs | the residency model's own accounting; its records for live and retiring arenas, ghosts and the running preparation stay charged until released at the owner's safe point (R1) |

- **Refusal sites changed:** the open admission (`decide`, `SubOpen`) now tests `arenaUse` (live + retiring + pending) instead of live + pending; the lease tests (`SubOpen`, `SubAttach`) use `leasesInUse`; `slotsHeldHere` counts pending acquisitions once. No other refusal site changed (views, clients and the model's own limits already counted every state).
- **Regression:** `Test.TerrainRuntime.Limits`: with two arenas allowed, a closed arena whose snapshot is borrowed still counts 2 of 2 and a third open is refused `BusyArenas`; with two outstanding borrowers on two closed arenas the open is refused until one borrower returns, then admitted while the other still retires; a pending open counts before it settles; a cancelled open counts nothing.
- **Extended randomized property (the owner's extension):** a fourth group of 24 seeds runs under tight limits (two arenas, five leases, two views, three clients). After every step, including steps taken inside a held borrow (where a closed arena is retiring), each limit's count must equal its independent enumeration and never exceed the limit; the group asserts it saw retiring arenas.
- **Negative controls (each broken once locally on a committed tree, run, reverted):** NC36 (arena limit ignores retiring arenas: the round-9 defect) fails the first example (2 of 2 expected, 1 seen); NC36b fails the property ("admission counts 1 but the entities holding it number 2"); NC38 (ignores pending opens) fails `counts a pending open before it settles`; NC39 (lease limit ignores pending acquisitions) fails the property on 24 of 24 seeds. NC37 (counting a settled acquisition twice) is equivalent: an open or attach leaves the table when it settles or ends, so that guard is defensive and unreachable.

## Round 11 follow-up (owner directive, round 12): expected usage from the service's own data

Round 11 (head `5856f0b8`, Codex, CHANGES_REQUESTED): the usage derivation summed the same model fields `bankLedger` sums, so a model record that disappears while the service still holds the resource was invisible to both.

- **Disclosure, one production addition, no behavior change:** `inspectCensusSTM` (with the `Census`, `ArenaCensus` and `RequestCensus` types) in `Runtime.hs`, a read-only STM diagnostic like `inspectLimits`. It was needed because the contents of a retiring arena were not readable through the public inspectors (`inspectArenaSTM` excludes `stateRetiring`). No existing code path was touched; no test exposed a defect.
- **What the service reports (from its own structures):** per arena, live and retiring, the accounted bytes its `Resident` value holds in each vertical region (the region's fixed metadata, an index entry per column and each segment's metadata and payload, measured on the data) and its retained-changes bytes; every request it holds (table and withdrawn ghosts) with whether it holds a removal reservation and a model record; its outstanding snapshots.
- **What the property compares after every step (inside held borrows too), in one transaction with the model:** each region's charge equals what its detail holds (a region that has had removals may be charged up to 64 bytes per removal above it, the model's conservative worst case); no region held by only one side (an evicting region excepted until its victim is released); edit bank occupied = the arenas' retained changes, reserved = the service's removals; staging request records and removal reservations = what the service's requests hold; command slots = the service's requests holding a model record; snapshot holds = the service's snapshots. The load jobs, interests, hold and view records and a transfer's duplicate exist only in the model and are taken from its entity lists (stated in `Usage.hs` and the README).
- **The reviewer's control is an example:** `notices a model that dropped its records while the service still holds the resources, in every bank` builds a model without the arena (an empty arena of the same identity) beside the service's real census and requires disagreements in the resident, edit (occupied and reserved), staging, command-slot and snapshot-hold rows.
- **One control per bank, against production code (each run once on a committed tree, reverted):** NC46 (resident bank charged nothing for detail: 77 of 93 examples fail, "model counts 0 but the service holds 676"); NC47 (edit bank ignores retained changes: 53, "0 but 64"); NC48 (edit bank ignores a removal's reservation: 89); NC49 (staging ignores request records: 93, "64 but 224"); NC50 (staging ignores a removal's staging reservation: 89, "258 but 770"); NC41-NC45 and NC36-NC40 from earlier rounds still fail their examples.
- **Flake disclosure:** one run in 170 full-suite runs at `+RTS -N4` failed in `Closure ... lets cancel, release and close proceed with the ordinary queue full` (`AlreadySettled` instead of `CancelWon`). That example is untouched, does not use the census, and was not reproduced in 400 runs alone or 600 runs of its group in four parallel workers.
