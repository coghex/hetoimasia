## 1. Recording the effect before cancellation can escape

### [#189] PRR-1. Protect presentation enqueue and obligation publication from cancellation

> **Captured note:** P2 — `presentWithFence` catches exceptions from the native
> presentation call, but publishes its `PresentAttempted` observation after that
> inner `try` has returned to an unmasked caller. Cancellation in between can
> leave an enqueued present absent from the journal. The later successful
> device-idle boundary then authorizes destruction without even probing that
> present's fence. Protect the effect-to-obligation handoff, and exercise that
> boundary through the same mechanism used by the native path.

**Verification:** The pinned tree has the following reachable failure path:

1. `Main.native` calls `runProof` without an enclosing mask. `runProof` and
   `catchAll` also leave the procedure unmasked; `catchAll` catches an escaping
   asynchronous exception as an unexpected stop and subsequently runs cleanup.
2. `presentWithFence` resets its present fence and calls `queuePresentKHR`
   inside `try @SomeException`. The present enqueues successfully.
3. The inner `try` returns. Before `observe ... (PresentAttempted ...)` writes
   the result journal, an asynchronous exception is delivered. This exception
   is outside that inner `try`, so the conservative `NotAResult` classification
   for exceptions thrown by the call is never recorded either.
4. `runProof` catches the exception and starts cleanup. `vkDeviceWaitIdle`
   succeeds and records `TeardownBoundaryReached Succeeded`.
5. `probePresentFences` derives its list exclusively from the journal's
   `standingPending`. The missing present is absent, so its maintenance fence
   is never checked. On a recycled slot, the earlier retired presentation's
   observation likewise leaves no pending entry for the new effect.
6. `dispositionOf` sees a held boundary and no pending presentation. The cleanup
   executor therefore destroys the present fence, presentation semaphore,
   swapchain and parents without presentation-retirement evidence.

A disposable Hspec check compiled warning-clean with GHC 9.14.1 verified that
the inner `try` returns `Unmasked`. A second example used `MVar` handshakes to
deliver `ThreadKilled` at a controlled post-return/pre-observation boundary:
the stand-in native enqueue had occurred, the outer exception handler observed
the cancellation, and the journal remained empty. Feeding that journal plus
`TeardownBoundaryReached Succeeded` to the actual pinned `Retention.decide`
returned `Destroy` for the present fence, presentation semaphore and swapchain,
with no retained handle. Both examples passed under `+RTS -N1`.

That experiment establishes the Haskell exception boundary and its consequence
for the real decision function. The scheduling hook was in a disposable
stand-in, not in the native implementation; it is not evidence that a driver
was interrupted or a Vulkan use-after-free was induced. The complete native
failure-path trace above supplies the connection to the production call site
inside this private qualification harness.

**Handoff context:**

- **Current behavior:** Recorded pending presents are retained correctly, but
  cancellation can prevent the newly enqueued present from being recorded at
  all. The original failing step remains a failed verdict, which cannot make
  subsequent unsafe destruction valid.
- **Expected behavior:** Once a presentation may have enqueued, cancellation
  must not escape without either publishing its obligation or preserving an
  equivalently conservative owned state. The original cancellation remains the
  primary failure; ordinary completion, device-loss permission, and unknown
  effects retain their distinct meanings. Keep comments precise about that
  protected handoff.
- **Scope and constraints:** Repair the private Vulkan proof's
  effect-to-obligation boundary, preserving the established retention decision,
  exact successful cleanup ordering, typed result classification, and no-effect
  out-of-memory cases. Do not make native calls preemptible, wrap destruction in
  a timeout, or move GPU waits into an uninterruptible CPU cleanup action. This
  is distinct from PR #186's correct acquisition ownership and its separate
  cancellation-test-strength finding.
- **Verification target:** Add deterministic headless Hspec coverage through
  the shared native bookkeeping mechanism for cancellation after native enqueue
  but before result publication, including a recycled slot. With device idle
  successful and the present fence still unresolved, assert no destruction of
  that presentation's resources or parents; retain the original failure and
  actual teardown observations. Exercise the protected boundary itself rather
  than only constructing an already-correct observation list, and ensure an
  unprotected-handoff regression is rejected without sleeps or probabilistic
  retries. The ordinary headless proof must continue to pass.
- **Deduplication:** The fresh open inventory contains only epics #49, #131,
  #140, #145 and #155. All-state searches for `presentation cancellation`,
  `present ledger` and `proof cancellation` found no repair covering this
  effect-publication gap. Closed #181 establishes the intended recording
  contract; closed #182 concerns allocation ownership. PR #185's claimed ledger
  row had no earlier review or report. The earlier PR #174 finding concerned
  recorded fence timeouts and the unconditional cleanup boundary, which this
  PR repairs; this entry concerns the distinct missing observation.
- **Remaining uncertainty:** No native cancellation frequency or platform
  reproduction is claimed. The defect is the verified unmasked handoff and the
  cleanup behavior reached when its asynchronous-delivery window is taken.
