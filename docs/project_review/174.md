## 1. Retirement after an unsuccessful proof step

### [#181] PRR-1. Preserve presentation retirement obligations when the proof stops

> **Captured note:** P2 — After a present-fence timeout, the proof substitutes
> `deviceWaitIdle` for the missing presentation-retirement evidence and then
> destroys the presentation fences, semaphores and swapchain. A timeout is not
> completion, and device idle does not supply the missing guarantee. Preserve
> pending obligations and their parents until valid retirement or an applicable
> device-loss destruction rule permits release.

**Verification:** The pinned source implements this reachable sequence:

1. `presentWithFence` enqueues a presentation with its maintenance fence and
   observes a successful presentation result.
2. `awaitFence` receives `TIMEOUT` after its finite wait and throws `Stop`.
   This is an explicitly handled result, not an invented exception inside the
   driver. No device loss has been established.
3. `runProof` converts that exception into `Stopped` and invokes the same
   cleanup stack as the successful path.
4. The cleanup boundary calls `deviceWaitIdle`. If it returns success, cleanup
   proceeds without rechecking or waiting for the pending present fence.
5. `destroySlots` destroys that fence and presentation semaphore, followed by
   swapchain destruction. No intervening operation established presentation
   retirement. A cleanup-boundary exception also does not gate those releases:
   `runCleanups` catches it and continues indiscriminately.

Khronos specifically documents that queue/device idle is insufficient to release
presentation resources and that maintenance-fence evidence supplies the missing
guarantee. The extension permits semaphore destruction after an associated
presentation fence signals, and swapchain destruction after the fences for all
past presents signal. This source trace therefore reaches destruction without
its required evidence even if device idle returns successfully. See the
[Khronos semaphore reuse guide](https://docs.vulkan.org/guide/latest/swapchain_semaphore_reuse.html#_vk_ext_swapchain_maintenance1_extension)
and [maintenance present-fence contract](https://docs.vulkan.org/refpages/latest/refpages/source/VkSwapchainPresentFenceInfoKHR.html).

**Evidence:**

- `tools/vulkan-proof/proof/Test/Vulkan/Proof/Run.hs:1231` enqueues the present;
  line 1252 waits for its fence. The slot's `slotPresented` flag is written only
  after that wait succeeds, so it is not an outstanding-operation record on
  the first failed wait.
- `tools/vulkan-proof/proof/Test/Vulkan/Proof/Run.hs:1222` rejects every fence
  wait result other than `SUCCESS`, including `TIMEOUT`.
- `tools/vulkan-proof/proof/Test/Vulkan/Proof/Run.hs:345` catches the stopped
  procedure and runs cleanup at line 352.
- `tools/vulkan-proof/proof/Test/Vulkan/Proof/Run.hs:637` registers swapchain
  and slot destruction; lines 645–647 register the later, therefore first-run,
  device-idle boundary. There is no presentation-retirement check in cleanup.
- `tools/vulkan-proof/proof/Test/Vulkan/Proof/Run.hs:1045` destroys the slots'
  present fences and semaphores. `runCleanups` at line 125 continues through
  every registered action even when the boundary itself fails.
- `docs/vulkan_backend_design.md:1358` requires retained ownership when safety
  cannot be established, and line 1380 distinguishes presentation completion
  from device idle. Q-2 at line 1621 explicitly forbids a device-idle teardown
  fallback and line 1625 retains parents when safety is unresolved.
- `tools/vulkan-proof/proof/Test/Vulkan/Proof/Spec.hs:251` assesses teardown
  after native actions have already run. Its assertions can reject a failed
  run, but cannot make an earlier unsafe destruction valid.

The same failure-path classification must account for a presentation rejected
by the presentation engine: out-of-date and surface-lost results still enqueue
the associated queue operations and semaphore waits under the
[presentation command contract](https://docs.vulkan.org/refpages/latest/refpages/source/vkQueuePresentKHR.html).
A non-success result alone is not permission to forget those effects.

**Handoff context:**

- **Current behavior:** A missing present-fence result stops the experiment,
  but not the destruction of resources that may still belong to that present.
  The blanket cleanup stack treats every registered release as independently
  safe, although these releases depend on a successfully established boundary.
- **Expected behavior:** Track native effects as they occur and retain the
  relevant presentation records and parents on timeout or uncertain safety.
  Distinguish proven completion from device-loss destruction permission and
  from an ordinary failure that establishes neither. A failed proof must still
  obey native lifetime requirements.
- **Scope and constraints:** Repair the qualification harness's unsuccessful
  exit paths and accompanying explanation. Preserve the main-thread rule,
  callback storage through final teardown, both successful abandonment paths,
  and present-fence-based normal recycling. Do not add unsafe destruction to
  force a bounded exit. Native calls cannot be made preemptible merely by a
  Haskell timeout; unresolved safety needs the accepted retention/external
  termination policy. The missing local watchdog is not a separate finding,
  and this report does not impose VK-8's later timing budget on VK-2.
- **Verification target:** Headless Hspec injection should cover successful
  present followed by fence timeout and device-idle success, and a non-device-loss
  failure of the cleanup boundary. In both cases dependent destruction must
  remain prohibited. Later valid present-fence evidence should permit ordered
  release. Test the separately justified device-loss disposition and enqueued
  presentation errors. Any renewed native evidence requires its applicable
  isolated-display or explicit per-session desktop authorization.
- **Deduplication:** The fresh open inventory contains only epics. All-state
  searches for `Vulkan teardown` and `present fence timeout` found no repair
  issue; #155 and closed #158 provide context. The claimed PR #174 ledger row
  has no previous review/report, and existing project-review reports describe
  no earlier instance of this proof failure path.
- **Remaining uncertainty:** No live driver was made to delay a fence or fail
  cleanup. The finding proves the source's response to those specified results;
  it does not claim an observed hang or failure in either retained native run.

## 2. Cleanup ownership before composite construction succeeds

### [#182] PRR-2. Retain cleanup ownership during partial native construction

> **Captured note:** P2 — The proof registers an entire pair of frame slots
> only after every child allocation succeeds, and never registers capture
> buffer/memory cleanup. A failure midway leaves already-created children
> outside the cleanup stack, which then destroys their device. Register or
> otherwise protect each successful acquisition before any later step can fail.

**Verification:** A complete synchronous allocation-failure trace exists at the
pin without relying on cancellation timing. The first `createSemaphore` in
`newSlot` succeeds; the second fails, for example with an allocation error.
The binding exception escapes `newSlot` and `newSlots`, so the `onExit` that
would own the slots is never reached. The already-registered device cleanup
still executes through `runProof`/`runCleanups`, with the first semaphore never
destroyed. The same gap loses all of the first slot if construction of the
second slot fails.

The native requirement is explicit: destroy/free all destroyable device children
before `vkDestroyDevice` (`VUID-vkDestroyDevice-device-05137`). A failed proof
verdict does not exempt its own teardown from that requirement. See the
[device destruction contract](https://docs.vulkan.org/refpages/latest/refpages/source/vkDestroyDevice.html).

**Evidence:**

- `tools/vulkan-proof/proof/Test/Vulkan/Proof/Run.hs:605` registers destruction
  of the logical device before any slots exist.
- `tools/vulkan-proof/proof/Test/Vulkan/Proof/Run.hs:640` constructs both slots
  before the only slot cleanup registration at line 641.
- `tools/vulkan-proof/proof/Test/Vulkan/Proof/Run.hs:1003` traverses two slot
  constructions. `newSlot` at line 1010 creates semaphores, fences and command
  storage sequentially, with no partial-construction cleanup owner.
- `tools/vulkan-proof/proof/Test/Vulkan/Proof/Run.hs:1468` begins capture buffer
  construction. Buffer creation, memory allocation/binding, acquisition,
  submission, mapping and presentation can fail before the only `freeMemory`
  and `destroyBuffer` calls near the successful end of this function. Neither
  handle is on the run's cleanup stack. For example, a memory-allocation error
  after buffer creation leaves that buffer alive when device cleanup runs.
- `tools/vulkan-proof/proof/Test/Vulkan/Proof/Run.hs:345` and line 125 catch the
  escaped failure and run only registered actions. They cannot discover handles
  that the failed constructors never transferred to cleanup ownership.
- `tools/vulkan-proof/proof/Test/Vulkan/Proof/Spec.hs:251` checks the reported
  release list and failures only after a fully completed procedure; it does not
  inject constructor failures or account for partially acquired children.

**Handoff context:**

- **Current behavior:** Allocation/probe failure can be followed by native
  parent destruction while successfully allocated children remain live. The
  harness's claim to tear down its complete session on either success or stop
  is not true for partial slot or capture construction.
- **Expected behavior:** Every successfully created handle has a cleanup owner
  before another fallible step executes. Partial construction disposes of the
  children already acquired, exactly once and in valid dependency order, while
  preserving the primary failure and any cleanup failures. Once work has been
  submitted, PRR-1's completion requirements continue to govern release.
- **Scope and constraints:** Repair ownership of construction intermediates in
  the private proof harness. Protect acquisition-to-registration handoffs from
  interruption as well as ordinary synchronous failures. Keep GPU waits and
  native destruction out of uninterruptible CPU release callbacks; do not turn
  this qualification harness into a public production backend.
- **Verification target:** Use a headless Hspec seam to fail each slot
  acquisition in turn, including the second slot, and capture steps after
  buffer and memory acquisition. Assert every successful acquisition retains
  exactly one cleanup owner, safe children release before their device, and
  submitted children remain retained when completion is absent. Add a normal
  success control; reject a test that merely fails the overall verdict while
  accepting leaked children.
- **Deduplication:** All-state searches for `Vulkan allocation cleanup`,
  `proof partial allocation` and `newSlots` found no dedicated repair issue.
  Closed #158 and #160 are the original proof/model work; existing resource
  construction work is context rather than a fix in this harness. The claimed
  PR #174 row has no earlier report, so this is new rather than a repeat.
- **Remaining uncertainty:** This is a verified source and native-contract
  trace. No actual allocator exhaustion or driver failure was induced; the
  retained successful runs do not exercise the failing construction prefixes.
