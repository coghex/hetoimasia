## 1. Attachment integration contract

### [#215] PRR-1. Align attachment callback restrictions with finite backend progress

> **Captured note:** P3 — The `AttachmentProtocol` introduction forbids every
> callback from making any GPU call. Reconcile that blanket prohibition with
> the accepted finite-progress contract and the protocol's ownership of
> dependent construction and disposal. Preserve the prohibition on blocking
> GPU waits, worker waits and native event pumping; do not direct an integration
> to acquire or dispose its dependents outside the protected attachment.

**Verification:** At the pin, the type's Haddock applies “None may ... make a
GPU call” to every callback, including `protocolConstruct` and
`protocolRollback`. Immediately above, `CompletionPolicy` instead specifies
finite, nonblocking progress and explicitly qualifies latency claims for native
calls. The maintained GLFW contract and accepted LIFE P-2 prohibit blocking GPU
waits, while Vulkan P-7 requires surface construction under the protected window
attachment and destruction before acknowledgement. A blanket ban on backend
calls is incompatible with that intended use of the supplied protocol.

The comment predates PR #170: `git blame -L 507,512 --
packages/glfw/runtime-glfw-core/Hetoimasia/Runtime/GLFW/Internal/Retirement.hs`
attributes it to `2a7f41c2b1f5a152b2b8359c332f34a34ed36984`, “Establish the
protected host retirement boundary.” PR #170 extends this same Haddock with
the metadata-demand contract and retains the contradictory introduction.

**Evidence:**

- `packages/glfw/runtime-glfw-core/Hetoimasia/Runtime/GLFW/Internal/Retirement.hs:509`
  applies the no-GPU-call restriction to every callback. Lines 534–543 define
  construction, rollback and retirement-step responsibilities.
- The same file at `:489` defines `CompletionPolicy` in terms of finite,
  nonblocking progress; lines 494–499 distinguish a bounded opportunity from
  a wall-clock guarantee about a native call and prohibit waiting on the GPU.
- `docs/glfw.md:3281` states that no opportunity performs a blocking GPU wait,
  with finite progress as the trusted backend's contract and native latency
  limitations explicitly documented.
- `docs/window_graphics_lifetime_design.md:187` fixes the accepted owner-thread,
  finite-progress rule and prohibition on blocking GPU waits during normal
  turns. Issue #144, requirement 5 and its approved clarification, repeat it.
- `docs/vulkan_backend_design.md:1058` requires surface creation under the
  protected window attachment and surface destruction before its attachment
  acknowledgement and instance release. The attachment owns the lifetime
  boundary that these effects must respect.

**Handoff context:**

- **Current behavior:** The public protocol's documentation forbids the backend
  calls needed to fulfill its own construction and retirement responsibilities,
  while its neighboring policy and authoritative contracts allow finite native
  progress under the ownership and scheduling rules.
- **Expected behavior:** Describe permitted construction/disposal and finite
  progress consistently, distinguishing them from forbidden blocking waits,
  event pumping and worker waits. Keep owner-thread execution, protected
  registration, independent retirement facts and backend latency limits clear.
- **Scope and constraints:** Correct the protocol comment and directly related
  wording. No Vulkan implementation, new capability, real GPU test, scheduling
  change or relaxation of safe retirement is required. This is an existing
  inconsistency discovered while reviewing the touched contract; the metadata
  containment repair remains valid.
- **Verification target:** Compare the revised Haddock with `CompletionPolicy`,
  LIFE P-2, `docs/glfw.md` and Vulkan P-7. A reader must be able to place finite
  backend construction, queries and safe disposal within their protected owner
  without violating the prose. No runtime test is needed for this correction.
- **Deduplication:** The post-claim row had no earlier report or history. Open
  inventory and all-state searches for `AttachmentProtocol GPU` and
  `finite retirement callback` found the completed lifetime/metadata issues and
  the open umbrella epics, but no issue tracking this wording correction.
  `docs/project_review_165-150.md#PRR-1` is the distinct, now-fixed metadata
  escape and is credited to #170, not repeated here.
- **Remaining uncertainty:** No native GPU behavior was exercised or inferred.
  The contradiction is established by the current written contracts and source
  history; later backend capability proofs remain their own work.
