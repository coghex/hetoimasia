## 1. Deterministic cancellation coverage

### [#190] PRR-1. Make the acquisition handoff cancellation regression reject an unprotected handoff

> **Captured note:** P3 — The construction cancellation example allows the first
> ownership transfer to complete before cancellation is requested. It therefore
> passes when the acquisition mask it is meant to protect is removed. Establish
> the protected handoff deterministically and make the example reject that
> missing-mask regression without depending on timing or repeated lucky runs.

**Verification:** At the pinned tree, the first fake acquisition creates a handle,
puts `reached`, and immediately returns. The worker can then execute `holding`'s
`writeIORef` and reach the second acquisition's empty `MVar` before the test
thread consumes `reached` and calls `throwTo`. No handshake establishes a pending
cancellation before that first ownership transfer. Cancellation at the later
blocked acquisition sees an already-owned first handle, with or without the
mask.

This was confirmed in a disposable copy outside the reviewed worktree. The only
source mutation was changing
`holding (Held _ ref) acquire = mask_ $ do` to
`holding (Held _ ref) acquire = do` in `Ownership.hs`. The existing example
passed after rebuilding, then passed 30 of 30 direct invocations with
`--headless --match cancellation +RTS -N1 -RTS`. The reviewed source remained
unchanged. These runs establish a false-green regression check; they are not
an estimate of how often an unprotected native handoff would fail.

**Evidence:**

- `tools/vulkan-proof/proof/Test/Vulkan/Proof/ConstructionSpec.hs:591` — the
  explanatory comment describes announcing that cancellation may be thrown,
  rather than observing that it is pending at the handoff.
- `tools/vulkan-proof/proof/Test/Vulkan/Proof/ConstructionSpec.hs:600` — the
  first acquisition creates the handle, signals `reached`, then returns without
  coordinating with the throwing thread.
- `tools/vulkan-proof/proof/Test/Vulkan/Proof/ConstructionSpec.hs:604` — only
  the second acquisition blocks. By then the first handoff may be complete.
- `tools/vulkan-proof/proof/Test/Vulkan/Proof/ConstructionSpec.hs:615` — the
  test forks construction, consumes the signal, and only then calls `throwTo`.
- `tools/vulkan-proof/proof/Test/Vulkan/Proof/Ownership.hs:137` — the actual
  `mask_` remains present and protects acquisition and registration. Removing
  precisely this protection did not make the supposed regression fail.
- Issue #182's approved review comment explicitly requires a deterministically
  coordinated cancellation-at-handoff acceptance case using the ownership
  mechanism shared with the native path.

**Handoff context:**

- **Current behavior:** The test reliably observes a cancelled construction
  and cleanup of its first handle, but may cancel only after that handle is
  already owned. Its success does not establish the promised handoff coverage.
- **Expected behavior:** Headless acceptance must distinguish the protected
  handoff from an unprotected one. Make the synchronization or explicit boundary
  assertions strong enough that removing the protection fails deterministically,
  while preserving exactly-once child cleanup before device cleanup. Do not add
  an interruptible wait after acquiring an unowned handle to production code.
- **Scope and constraints:** This is a private proof-harness test follow-up.
  The current implementation's mask is correct; no new production lifecycle
  policy, native window session, or broader resource abstraction is needed.
  Keep related test comments accurate and avoid sleeps or probability-based
  assertions.
- **Verification target:** Run the existing headless proof normally and the
  focused cancellation example on the corrected source; verify that a scratch
  mutation removing the acquisition mask is rejected. Exercise the shared
  ownership mechanism and keep the test safe under a single RTS capability.
- **Deduplication:** The open inventory contains only epics #49, #131, #140,
  #145 and #155. All-state searches for `cancellation ownership` and
  `handoff regression` found no follow-up for this coverage gap. Closed #182
  supplies the unmet deterministic acceptance requirement. PR #186's claimed
  ledger row has no earlier review or report.
- **Remaining uncertainty:** No production cancellation leak was demonstrated
  at the pin. The finding is confined to the test's verified inability to reject
  an unprotected handoff under the observed schedule.
