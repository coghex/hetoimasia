## Findings

### GUIDE-1. Keep subprocess-group identity reserved through cleanup signals

**Disposition:** repair. **Verification:** static control-flow and OS-contract risk; no unrelated process was signalled and no PID-reuse experiment ran. **Principles:** V-3, V-10; #588's accepted no-unreserved-identifier amendment, comment 6569447216.

**Evidence:** [Subprocess.hs:978](https://github.com/coghex/hetoimasia/blob/b800f2fe9f81e3daaa53222759293fd17b97d04d/tools/test-support/src/Test/Support/Subprocess.hs#L978) reaps the direct child before drain and release. `endGroup` at lines 744–782 probes the numeric group and signals it when present; `probeFor` at 789–797 remembers only an absence it actually observed. [OwnedSubprocess.hs:601](https://github.com/coghex/hetoimasia/blob/b800f2fe9f81e3daaa53222759293fd17b97d04d/tools/test/OwnedSubprocess.hs#L601) tests absence **before** reuse.

**Consequence:** the original leader can be reaped, its group empty, and the number reused before the first cleanup probe. That probe sees the unrelated group and cleanup sends it TERM/KILL. A positive probe also cannot reserve an identity until the later signal. This inference follows the code and the [kill](https://man7.org/linux/man-pages/man2/kill.2.html), [waitpid](https://man7.org/linux/man-pages/man2/waitpid.2.html) and [identifier lifetime](https://docs.oracle.com/cd/E19620-01/805-3174/6j31do6c5/index.html) contracts; frequency is unmeasured.

**Action:** preserve a stable reservation through every possible signal, or use another race-free ownership mechanism. A second existence probe is insufficient. Preserve descendant cleanup, bounded observation, reaping and failure evidence. Closed #588/#603 fixed the distinct observed-absence-then-reuse schedule; no open issue covers this residual case. Current-head recheck found the relevant code unchanged.

Labels: none proposed.

```markdown
## Background

At master b800f2fe, Test.Support.Subprocess reaps a run-to-completion child before drain and scope release (tools/test-support/src/Test/Support/Subprocess.hs:978). endGroup:744–782 subsequently probes and signals its numeric process-group ID. probeFor:789–797 latches an observed absence, but cannot distinguish a replacement group before that observation or reserve an identity between a positive probe and the signal.

The concrete schedule is: leader reaped; original group empties; identifier reused; first cleanup probe sees the replacement; cleanup signals an unrelated group. This is a static ownership risk, not an observed unrelated-process kill. The existing regression at tools/test/OwnedSubprocess.hs:601 covers absence before reuse, a different schedule. #588's accepted amendment requires signals only while the identifier remains reserved.

## Requirements

1. Every TERM/KILL sent by the shared subprocess facility must address the owned process/group identity. Preserve ownership through the final possible signal; an existence check alone is not sufficient.
2. Preserve cleanup of owned descendants after leader exit, direct-child reaping, bounded cleanup observation, pipe retirement and primary/cleanup failure reporting. Keep one shared facility for its current consumers.
3. Add deterministic regressions for reuse before the first absence observation and loss of the last original member between a positive probe and a signal. Their negative controls must reject the old behavior without signalling an unrelated real process. Retain the existing observed-absence regression.
4. Update the owning source contract to state how identity stays owned and what happens when cleanup cannot be confirmed. Do not weaken a failure into success or introduce a broad process-name kill.

The new safe seam regressions and existing subprocess ownership/failure cases pass. Complete the planner-selected required groups. No desktop launch or real unrelated-process reuse experiment is required.

## Out of scope

Application runtime watchdog policy, unrelated process supervisors, PID-churn benchmarking, broad test cleanup refactors and implementation of later guide findings.
```
